Read this first

The product works. The people are the problem.

The loop is complete: record or write a reply, the server renders it, Gemini transcribes it, labels FOR or AGAINST and scores its claims, and people browse a ranked grid, swipe between sides, search, follow, send each other videos and react. More than 650 automated tests pass across the app and the server. What is missing is not another feature. It is a room with enough people in it that every reply gets an answer.

The main risk

A debate product needs a second person for every post. With 10 to 15 users most replies will go unanswered, and an unanswered video feels like shouting into a void. People leave after one or two such experiences. Almost every other risk is smaller than this one.

What I recommend

Stop adding surface area. Pick one campus and one recurring use, make sure every new post gets an answer within hours, and fix the three things that stop people reaching each other: notifications, public links, and onboarding.

2 · The thing that kills social apps

The environment problem, in three parts

When people say a social app fails because of its environment, they usually mean one of three different things. They need different fixes, so it helps to keep them apart.

The room is empty

Not enough people, so nothing new appears and nobody answers. This is the cold-start problem, and it is the one that ends most new apps.

The room is hostile

Enough people, but the tone is bad. Comments turn into fights, newcomers are mocked, and the thoughtful people leave first.

The room does not fit life

People mean to use it but never form the habit. No reason to open it today, so the tenth open never comes.

Why FOREST is more exposed than most

  • Every reply needs a second person. A photo app is useful with zero followers. A debate is not.
  • The effort is high. A video reply costs far more than a like, so people post less, so there is less to answer.
  • The topics are risky. Politics under your real phone number, in front of classmates, makes quiet people stay quiet.
  • The best feature needs both sides. The FOR and AGAINST swipe only shows its value once a debate has people on each side.

What worked for others, and what to copy

These are widely reported stories, told here only for the lesson in each. Check the details before quoting any of them outside the team.

ProductWhat it didThe lesson for FOREST
FacebookOpened one university at a time before opening to everyone.A small dense network feels alive. A large thin one feels dead. Finish one campus before the next.
InstagramLaunched as one thing, photos with filters, that was worth using alone.Give people a reason to use the product before anyone else is there. For us that is the editor and the AI labelling of your own take.
Tinder, BeRealGrew through campus groups and ambassadors, one cohort at a time.Societies and classes are a distribution channel, not just users.
ClubhouseInvite-only scarcity created a rush, then faded.Scarcity gets the first visit. Only a reason to come back keeps people. Plan the habit, not just the launch.

7 · The playbook

How to break the environment problem

There is no trick. The pattern behind most apps that got through is the same: make a very small place feel full, give people a reason to use it before others arrive, and give them a reason to come back tomorrow.

Fourteen ideas, strongest first

The other environment: tone

A full room can still be a bad room. Structure helps, because arguing FOR or AGAINST is more civil than a free-for-all, but it can also turn into a contest to win. Build the tone on purpose.

Say the rule once, where people see it

"Argue with the claim, not the person." On the reply screen, in one line, not in a policy page.

Offer a steelman prompt

Before replying, an optional "say their point in one sentence". People who do it argue better, and the other person feels heard.

Weight visibility by credibility

Posts with sound claims travel further than posts with loud ones. This is already the stated rule for rewards.

Slow down new accounts

Fewer replies per hour at first, a short wait before replying to strangers, and faster review of anything reported from a new account.

Close the report loop quickly

A person who reports and sees it handled in a day trusts the room. Show the outcome to the reporter.

Let people mute a topic

Political exhaustion is real. A mute control keeps heavy users from quitting entirely.

A ninety-day plan for one campus

Weeks 0 to 2 · Make it ready

Notifications, public debate pages, onboarding, analytics events, Play internal testing, a real-phone speed pass. Interview ten people.

Weeks 3 to 4 · Seed

Forty founding members from friends and two societies. Three prompts. A small group of real volunteers who commit to answer new posts within hours.

Weeks 5 to 8 · Make it dense

Motion rooms with societies, one live debate a week, the reply-wanted queue, invite flow. Watch the numbers weekly.

Weeks 9 to 12 · Prove it

Decide with data. Open a second campus only if the gate below is met. If it is not, fix the product before spending more on people.

10 · Before building more

Talk to ten people first

Two weeks of interviews will change the order of this report. Ten people is enough to hear the same thing three times.

Who

  • Three college students, different years
  • Two people looking for work
  • Two society or club leads
  • One lecturer
  • One Punjabi or Hindi first-language user
  • One working professional who reads politics

Ask about the past, not the future

  • When did you last argue about something that mattered, and where?
  • What made you stop, or never start?
  • Last time you posted an opinion online, what happened next?
  • Show the app for five minutes and say nothing. Where do they get stuck?
  • What would make you send it to one friend?

Three cheap experiments

  • Manual motion. Run one debate in a WhatsApp group, collect videos by hand, post them to FOREST. See who answers.
  • Fake door. Share a public page for a debate before the real one exists and count who taps "join".
  • Reply guarantee. For one week answer every new post within hours. See whether people come back.

What would change the plan

  • Nobody wants to be on video: lead with voice and text.
  • Students will not post politics under their own name: add topic spaces that are not political first.
  • Societies are keen: build motion rooms before onboarding.
  • Job-seekers want recognition: start the credibility record earlier.
11 · For the two of you

Decisions that are yours

  1. Which one campus first, and which one recurring use. Placements, exams, campus elections and society motions are all plausible. Pick one for the pilot.
  2. Age policy. Stay 18+ through the beta, or invest in a parental-consent flow. I recommend staying 18+.
  3. Stories. Hide the placeholder now and decide on reposts later, or build them. I recommend hiding.
  4. Seed accounts. Labelled bots are allowed by your rules. How many, for how long, and who are the real volunteers who will answer people?
  5. Creator money. The README plans a paid creator programme. Start it before or after credibility is trusted? I recommend after.
  6. Public pages. Are public debate pages indexable by search engines, or only reachable by link? It affects growth and privacy.
  7. Name. Keep FOREST or change it before the first store listing.
  8. What counts as success after 90 days. Write the numbers down now.
